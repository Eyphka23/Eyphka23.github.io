With the deadline at hand, our classmates were always ready to lend a helping hand to each other to handle the difficult problem.

Sometimes, students would hang around the school gate after class, or hang out in the park. Yesterday, Tom skipped lessons and hung around the harbour with some other boys. When he climbed a fence, Leo shouted, “Hang on to the railing so you don’t fall!” We wondered what had happened to him because he looked so upset. It happened that Tom lost his key, and it also happened to be raining at that moment. It so happens that there are 20 students in our class, so we all came to his help.

Hardly had the teacher entered the classroom when we sat down. Hardly anyone has bothered to complain about the extra work, and hardly can you expect our class to fail. We were ready to endure great hardships together. We knew that conflicts would do harm to our teamwork and be harmful to our friendship, so we always tried to live in harmony with each other.

During the preparation, the teacher had us write a report and had the classroom decorated. However, she can’t have students talking loudly during the quiet study hour. Working all day made us tired, so we were glad to have a day off. By the end of the day, we were covered from head to foot in mud from making stage props. When things got messy, Leo told us not to lose our heads. He noticed I had my head in the clouds and was daydreaming, so he said, “Let’s put our heads together to find a solution, and we will keep our heads above water.”

Our project was so creative that we hoped it would grab the headlines and cause a sensation. We tried to learn the key definitions by heart, promised not to lose heart, and dedicated our heart and soul to the task. From the bottom of my heart, I really appreciated this experience. After a heated discussion about our presentation, it began to rain heavily. We can’t help doing a happy dance when we finished, and we can’t help but feel proud. I was happy to help my friends with their parts, help them out of tight schedules, and help them through the difficult week. On the final table, there were snacks; the teacher smiled and said, “Please help yourselves to the cakes, don’t be polite!”

We saw books scattered here and there. “Here and now, let’s toast to our success! Here’s to our wonderful team!” Leo said. Although we might hesitate to speak in public or hesitate over which topic to choose, we finally completed the presentation without hesitation. The principal spoke highly of our project, and we didn’t hide our joy from him. When Leo dropped a hint about a celebration party, the teacher took the hint and agreed. This success will make history in our school and go down in history as the best project ever. No difficulties could hold back our progress. The teacher told us to hold on to our dreams, hold out until the end, and hold up our hands if we had questions, ensuring nothing would hold everyone up again.

3. A Global Exchange

My friend David is a famous cultural researcher who is well-known both at home and abroad. Last month, when I visited him, he warmly said, “Feel at home,” and asked me to make myself at home. His house is filled with traditional art, and it feels like a museum. David also volunteers at a home for the aged (also known as a nursing home). He told me that his city is home to over two million people. During our conversation, we discussed the challenges of modern life, and his words really hit home when he mentioned how lonely elderly people can get.

To relax, David decided to develop a hobby and take up a hobby—specifically, painting. To be honest, he isn’t very good at it yet, but it keeps him happy. Last week, he hosted a charity dinner in honor of the retired teachers and in memory of the school’s founders. A grand banquet was given in honor of the distinguished guests. Many students who graduate with honors attended the dinner, and the former principal was the guest of honour. David said, “I feel it an honor to stay with you, and I am honored to do something for our community.” The school board decided to honour David with a medal for his scientific work and civic contributions.

We carried out this project in the hope of raising money for charity and in the hope that more people would join us. David always told me to hope for the best and prepare for the worst. This experience helped broaden my horizons, especially since major social changes are now on the horizon. Before this, I used to have a horror of public speaking, but I overcame it.

In the charity committee, an unexpected student became a dark horse by leading the fundraising team. He didn’t want to put the cart before the horse by spending money before raising it. As the old proverb goes, “You can lead a horse to water, but you can’t make it drink.”

Our city wants to be a host country and host the Olympics in the future. To prepare, the local subway system now charges workers by the hour. Drivers are encouraged to keep early hours and keep good hours to avoid the rush hour traffic. Sometimes, volunteers work after hours—even in the early hours, in the small hours, or in the wee hours of the morning.

David has a great sense of humor, though he knows when to be serious. He once read a news story about terrorists who tried to take innocent people hostage, and in his humble opinion, security must be improved. In his free time, he doesn’t go hunting in the woods; instead, he prefers walking. Once, he saw an old man fall and had to hurry the old man to the hospital. He shouted to the bystanders, “Hurry up!” because he was in a hurry to save the man. He was careful not to hurt the patient’s feelings while keeping him calm.

Managing a large charity can feel like skating on thin ice. For an unelected prime minister, Mr. Monti was skating on thin ice as well. To make things easier, David told a funny joke to break the ice at the meeting. Talking of good ideas, here’s one to break the ice at a wedding reception.

Later, David came up with an idea to build a community library. We didn’t have a clear ideal of the plan or a general ideal of the budget at first. However, we learned not to identify wealth with happiness. Instead, we tried to identify the problem with the building and identify the link between diet and cancer for our health exhibits. I really identify with the main character in David’s favorite book because I can sympathize with his struggles and relate to his feelings.

We decided that, if necessary, we would change the plan, and if possible, we would expand the library. If only we had more space, it would be wonderful. If so, we will buy more bookshelves; if not, we will use digital screens. We can succeed only if we work together. We promised never to speak ill of our teammates or think ill of our partners.

In the beginning, I was ignorant of the local laws and was ignorant about the budget events. At that time, I was ignorant of events going on elsewhere. To fix this, we needed to be the image of a professional team and improve our public image. I could imagine doing this work for years and imagine the project to be a huge success. We even created an imaginary character (a fictional mascot) for the library.

To explain the project to the public, David decided to illustrate the plan with detailed charts. He noted that many people who immigrate into a country face difficulties. The library would have a positive impact on their lives. We know that a bomb explodes on impact, but education makes a peaceful impact. We want to have an impact on the community and make a profound impact on the children. The speech made a profound impact on the audience, and we knew that lack of funding impacted greatly on our childhood years.

Therefore, the city council decided to implement the new policy to support us. We will import books from foreign publishers to fill the shelves. David wanted to impress the importance of reading on the children and impress the visitors with our collection, leaving a lasting memory for everyone.

4. The Community App Project

When our team started building the community app, we wanted to make a good impression on the school principal. We noticed that old local historical archives were completely accessible to the public online, but the physical files in the ancient archive room were inaccessible. Furthermore, we realized that the language of teenagers is often completely inaccessible to adults, making digital collaboration between generations quite difficult. As high school students, we wanted our project to be independent of our parents financially. Unfortunately, during the first week of coding, a computer virus managed to infect our main server with malware, and soon every backup computer was infected with the digital disease.

We had to infer the cause of the leak from the system logs. To handle this crisis, our leader promised to keep the principal informed of our progress and inform the team members about the new security protocols. We knew this initial setback would have a major influence on our development timeline.

The coding quality of our initial software version was inferior to the professional standard in security, though our graphic design was clearly superior. Mr. Green, our IT advisor, came to inquire after our team’s well-being, inquire about our technical difficulties, and inquire into the source of the server error. He managed to inquire the truth of the situation of our team leader. Mr. Green gave us a deep insight into user experience design. He insisted on our redesigning the user interface, and insisted that we should be present at every review meeting. When one programmer made a mistake, he insisted that he was innocent and didn’t mean to break the code. Mr. Green didn’t get angry; instead, he tried to inspire us to think outside the box. He warned us never to insult anyone’s effort or shout insults at a teammate during disagreements.

To protect our new physical lab equipment, the school board decided to insure the computer room against fire. Our main goal was to integrate our database with the existing school system so that younger students could easily integrate into the high school way of life. We intend to launch the application next month, and the funding we received was intended for the development of digital learning.

At first, many local residents were interested in the concept, while a few adults have no interest in downloading a new application. However, in the interests of public education, we knew we had to push forward and not let minor doubts interfere with our goals or others. To promote our work, we introduced our software to the local community and decided to invest heavily in online advertising. We even invited the neighbors to our launch party.

The final phase of development involves gathering and analyzing user data. Our volunteers involved themselves in community outreach, though we warned them not to get involved in local political arguments. To protect privacy, we had to isolate personal data from public profiles.

The main topic at issue among the developers was how to handle user data, which had become a highly sensitive and controversial issue. Some beta testers have issues with the font size, and our lead designer took issue with the engineers about the layout choices. Before the city council could issue the official license, there were still a few details that needed ironing out.

Surprisingly, once the final patch was installed, the application started by itself in a few seconds. The main server now stands by itself in an acre of land where the school tech center is located. Although the project, in itself, was a complex problem to solve, the app now has the whole local market to itself. Throughout these stressful days, our project manager was patience itself, demonstrating that ultimate success comes from steady dedication.
